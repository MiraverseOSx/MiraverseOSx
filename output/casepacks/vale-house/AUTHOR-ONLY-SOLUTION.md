# Author-only solution

**Spoilers. Do not include this file in the investigator's opening packet.**

## What happened

Mara Vey visited Lily's house under cover of the 08:15 records appointment. Lily's reporting threatened to expose occupancy claims Mara had approved for a site no longer housing residents. The scope of any wider financial wrongdoing remains a follow-up inquiry; Mara acts to protect her own approvals and position.

Julian was present because Lily had asked him to compare dates. His visit was not an unannounced attempt to take Stella. Lily and Julian were separated, and Santiago was her current partner. There were personal tensions, but those tensions did not cause this incident.

During the discussion, Lily said another copy of the material was already set aside. Mara demanded the copies and tried to take the reporting folder. When Lily refused, Mara struck and pushed her during the confrontation; Lily hit her head and fell. The opening scene record does not establish the exact contact sequence. Later accounts from Lily and Julian support this reconstruction.

Mara threatened further harm and insisted that Julian and Stella leave with her. Julian complied to keep Stella safe. He drove Northline's NF-17 with Mara in the front passenger seat and Stella in the back. A camera image of him driving is therefore not proof that he organized a voluntary departure.

At 08:28, under pressure, Julian sent the message naming Northgate. The message's destination was false. Mara directed him to Cedar Annex R-4, where she kept Julian and Stella in a closed former accommodation office while she returned to Northline to submit the reconciliation bundle and deal with the remaining copies. The pack does not provide technical instructions for confinement or evasion.

Dena discovered Lily afterward and called for help. The folder and reporting envelope Mara failed to recover provide a contractor lead and an independent motive trail. Julian and Stella remain at Cedar Annex until the recovery response.

## Why the certificate matters

Do not turn missing years or registry wording into a forgery subplot. The author has accepted the certificate.

## Recovery scene

The investigating authority follows the supported R-4 location lead promptly. At Cedar Annex, staff initially refer to the family as a "temporary hold" under a liaison booking. There is no corresponding resident transfer order or scheduled placement.

Julian and Stella are located alive. Stella is physically unharmed in this version. Julian asks first whether Lily is alive. Once separated from contractor staff, he explains that leaving the house and sending the Northgate message were coerced.

Preserve the booking records and identify the staff who received the family. Do not automatically treat every staff member as an accomplice: establish who knew what and whether anyone prevented departure. Mara's responsibility is supported by her own actions and the evidence; other roles remain to be assessed.

Mara's later confrontation with the records can result in a partial admission that she "needed time" and believed the copies could still be retrieved. A confession is optional. The investigator should be able to locate the family and challenge her account without one.

## Proposed final finding

Mara Vey's records appointment provided access to the household. Lily's material connects Vey to disputed occupancy approvals and gives a reason to suppress the planned article. Vehicle and premises records contradict Vey's account and establish a practical location lead. Recovery and separate accounts establish coercion and strengthen attribution of the assault.

The investigator can recommend proceedings concerning Lily's assault and the detention of Julian and Stella, using the terminology of your world's authority. The narrative does not need a real-world legal ruling or statute number.

## Do not overclaim

- The marriage certificate is not a custody order.
- Julian's absence is not evidence of guilt by itself.
- Santiago's relationship is not a motive established by evidence.
- A message from a phone does not establish free intention.
- The initial medical note does not identify an attacker.
- An office submission timestamp is not proof its author was at the office all morning.
- The story does not prove a broad government conspiracy or the guilt of all Northline staff.
- A supported location lead can justify advancing the welfare response before a complete narrative is assembled.
