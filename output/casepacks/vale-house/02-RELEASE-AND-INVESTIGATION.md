# Release and investigation guide

For the case runner. This page describes document order but does not replace the author-only solution.

## Opening packet

Supply the opening case file and E01. Available facts: Lily was found injured, Julian and Stella are unaccounted for, and Dena called for help. Give no confirmed offender name and no unsupported diagnosis.

## First inquiries

| Investigator action | Release | What it can establish |
|---|---|---|
| Examine the house and the item log | E03 | An unidentified additional visitor is plausible; entry was not visibly forced. |
| Establish the family relationships | E02 certificate; E04; E05 | Julian and Lily's historical marriage, current separation, Santiago's current relationship, and a planned visit. |
| Ask Santiago for the message and his morning whereabouts | E04 and E05 | A message exists; his workplace account has independent support. |
| Ask why Julian visited and whether his employer sent him | E07 | Lily invited him; there was no employer-issued household collection order. |
| Obtain Lily's available account through the appropriate liaison | E06 | A contractor visit and disputed paperwork, with memory limitations. |
| Review the material Lily was preparing | E11 | A specific reporting motive and a records liaison named Mara Vey. |

Do not withhold ordinary identity information to force a preferred question. If the investigator makes a sensible request that would locate a document, provide it.

## Follow the contractor lead

- E03 and E11 identify Northline without requiring a perfect recollection from Lily.
- A request for the day's visitor appointment and vehicle movement records produces E08.
- A request to identify the location code on the movement record produces E09. The code and register must agree: **R-4 = Cedar Annex, Unit 4**.
- An interview with the records liaison produces E10. Her account is evidence to test, not a narrator-certified history.

The location lead is sufficiently concrete for a welfare/recovery response under the story's investigating authority. The investigator does not need to extract a confession first. Do not turn urgency into a procedural guessing contest.

## Interviews

Start with open questions, then let the investigator compare sources:

- Dena: What did you see, what did you hear, and what did you assume?
- Santiago: Who arranged the morning visit, when did the message arrive, and who can verify your whereabouts?
- Lily: Who was expected, what was being discussed, and which recollections are uncertain?
- Mara: Why did you visit, which vehicle did you use, where did it go, and which authority ordered any transfer?

## Fair alternative explanations

- Julian may initially appear to have left after an argument. E04's message is real but its account of intention is unverified.
- Santiago may initially appear relevant because of the relationship. E05 provides a practical way to assess that lead.
- An unforced door can mean an admitted visitor; it does not prove that the family consented to everything that followed.
- A contractor label establishes an association to examine. It does not by itself identify its wearer or make a transfer lawful.

## Recovery and report

Once the investigator identifies Cedar Annex, use the recovery facts in the author-only solution. Ask for a report that distinguishes:

1. observed or recorded facts;
2. reasonable inferences;
3. later corroboration after recovery;
4. questions still open.

A sound report can locate the missing people and identify Mara's role while leaving the precise injury mechanism or full financial extent unresolved.

## Optional difficulty adjustments

- **Short case:** release E03-E05 together and provide E08-E09 as one records response. About one investigation session.
- **Standard case:** separate the family, clinical, employer, reporting, transport, and premises inquiries.
- **Long case:** add other Northline sites and witnesses only after the family is recovered. Do not prolong the child's disappearance merely to extend play.
