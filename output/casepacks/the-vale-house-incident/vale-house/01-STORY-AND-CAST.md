# Story and cast

## Title

**The Vale House Incident**

Alternate presentation title: **The Door Left Open**.

## Opening

Dena Banks knows the sound of Lily and Stella leaving for the day. On the incident morning, the expected departure does not happen. A call goes unanswered. Dena finds the front door ajar, enters, and discovers Lily on the kitchen floor with a head injury.

Stella is missing. So is Julian, Lily's separated husband, who visited that morning. Santiago, Lily's current partner, receives a short message from Julian's number: "Taking Stella to Northgate. Don't follow us." At first glance, it looks like a custody dispute that became violent.

That explanation has to survive three questions: did Julian leave freely, why did the car travel away from Northgate, and why did a relocation contractor want to speak to Lily before her reporting meeting?

## What the investigator is trying to establish

1. What happened to Lily inside the house?
2. Where are Julian and Stella, and are they safe?
3. Who arranged their departure?
4. Was the message from Julian a reliable account of what he intended?
5. What does Lily's work have to do with the incident?

The immediate task is to locate the missing people. The investigator need not finish a perfect reconstruction before acting on a supported location lead.

## Cast

### Lily Preyana Moss

Travel journalist; injured resident; separated from Julian; Santiago's current partner. She still uses Moss professionally. The marriage certificate records Vale as the surname after marriage; the two names are not treated as two people.

Lily has been investigating why temporary accommodation sites were described as occupied after residents had already been moved out. Her writing began as travel and local-life reporting. Interviews with displaced residents led her to Northline's records. She wants documentary corroboration before publishing.

At first, Lily's account is limited by her injury and fragmented recollection. Later clarification is useful, but her memory does not identify the offender for the investigator before other evidence is gathered.

### Julian Denise Vale

Security administrator; Lily's separated husband; proposed father of Stella. He and Lily have unresolved personal tensions, but the pack does not establish a custody order or a legal finding about either parent. He visited at Lily's request to help interpret records and discuss that morning's arrangements.

Julian understands access records and knows their limits. A departure recorded on camera does not tell the observer whether the driver was acting freely. He has a reason to protect Stella and a reason to avoid provoking the person controlling their movement.

### Stella Vale

Missing child; proposed daughter of Lily and Julian. Her exact age remains an author field. She is found physically unharmed in the proposed resolution.

She is a person to locate and protect, not an interrogation puzzle. Adult statements and records carry the investigative solution. A later, brief account can corroborate that they were not allowed to leave; no distressing child testimony is needed to unlock the case.

### Dena Banks

Neighbor and discovering witness. Observant about routine, uncertain about anything she did not actually see. She can describe finding Lily and hearing a raised voice earlier. She cannot identify the speaker or the departing car's occupants.

Dena's useful distinction: "I thought it was a family argument. I didn't see who was arguing." Her first interpretation must not become an established fact.

### Santiago Whitmore

Lily's current romantic partner. He is not presented as her husband. Lily and Julian's separation is known, rather than a secret affair invented to explain the incident.

He works at a transit depot in this proposed story. His relationship gives investigators a reason to ask questions, not a reason to presume guilt. His shift records and a workplace witness help test his account. He supplies the message he received and agrees that its wording is unlike Julian's usual arrangements.

### Mara Vey

Newly authored character. Northline's records liaison and scheduling coordinator. She is the initial contact named in Lily's appointment notes, not introduced as a known offender.

She knows that Lily has found discrepancies between billed occupancy and actual moves. Her own approvals appear in the disputed records. She attempts to recover Lily's copies and stop the planned meeting. Her claim that her visit was routine is tested against transport and interview records.

### Northline Relocation Services

Newly authored private contractor operating temporary accommodation and relocation transport. Mara's acts should not be treated as proof that every employee or the public authority employing the company is involved.

## Tone

A contemporary civic investigation within Meridion: domestic uncertainty, competing accounts, a small institutional fraud, and a time-sensitive search. The marriage certificate establishes a family history. It is not a secret code, a forged exhibit, or evidence of guilt.
